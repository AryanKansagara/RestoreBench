# Research report: learned dense image reconstruction

## Question

Can a compact convolutional residual estimator improve pixel reconstruction over a fixed interpolation baseline on a controlled small-image task? This tests an executable data-to-model-to-evaluation workflow, not novelty in super-resolution research.

The targets are real digit images normalized to [0,1]. Inputs are generated by area-downsampling from 8x8 to 4x4. The CNN receives the bicubic-upsampled image and predicts a residual at every output pixel. Architecture: 1-to-32 convolution, ReLU, 32-to-32 convolution, ReLU, 32-to-1 convolution, all 3x3 with padding 1. The final convolution is initialized to zero. Total parameters: 9,857.

The fixed recipe uses seed 31, 40 epochs, batches of 64, MSE, AdamW (learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.001). Horizontal flips are applied to training targets before constructing the corresponding low-resolution inputs, so augmentation remains paired. Checkpoint selection uses validation MSE after clipping reconstructions to [0,1]. The random stratified train/validation/test split contains 1,077/270/450 images.

## Observed result

Executed 2026-10-27, macOS ARM64 CPU, Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.10.0. Epoch 40 had the lowest validation MSE.

| Measure on 450 test images | Bicubic | CNN |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mean per-image PSNR, range 1 | 13.5368 dB | 17.0505 dB |
| Global mean MSE | 0.0462346 | 0.0216300 |

Mean per-image PSNR improvement was 3.5137 dB, with positive gains on 446 images and non-improvement on four. The test subset was evaluated once after the fixed recipe and validation-based checkpoint selection. Twelve tests passed. `results/metrics.json` includes the validation-loss history; `results/evaluation.png` shows baseline-versus-learned PSNR and the per-image gain distribution.

## Interpretation and limits

The learned prior helps this particular digit reconstruction task. It does not demonstrate preserved fine detail on natural scenes, novel texture synthesis, perceptual realism, speedup or device performance. MSE/PSNR reward pixel fidelity, not semantic or perceptual quality. Bicubic is a useful baseline but not a comparison against modern restoration algorithms.

The split is not writer-disjoint and only one seed is used. Training augmentation and residual initialization have no ablation here; no causal benefit is assigned to either. The original dataset is tiny and familiar, and downsampling is synthetic. Claims about large-scale graphics ML, product deployment, patents or conference publications would not be supported by this experiment.

Next work: repeated seeds, stronger interpolation/restoration baselines, source-disjoint natural-image data with reviewed licensing, realistic degradation, perceptual metrics and target-device profiling. These are future extensions, not implemented features.
